04Why it fails quietly

Most automations don’t break on day one. They break on a Tuesday in March, and nobody notices.

Apps update, APIs change, AI models drift. Without someone watching, a broken flow can run for days before a customer tells you.

  • Silent failure

    It stopped, and nobody noticed

    A workflow fails at 3am. Orders don’t sync, emails don’t send, and you find out from an angry customer.

  • No handover

    Only one freelancer understands it

  • Untested AI

    The AI agent says things it shouldn’t

    Wrong refund rules, invented policies, missed handovers. It was never tested against real customer messages.

07Case studies

Our first client project in full, plus two typical projects.

Client project · Easi Breezi

They were about to hire. After we automated and simplified the flow, they didn’t need to.

0New hires needed
~5Steps cut from the flow
1Person can run the operation
Problem
Easi Breezi’s founder had no time left to develop new versions of the product, so they planned to hire someone for logistics and installation. Some work was already automated, but gaps were still handled by hand, and customers in different countries got a different experience, which was hard to manage and sustain.
What we found
Reviewing the whole operation, we found steps that weren’t worth automating at all. We also found that some information and guides meant for customers were failing silently: nobody knew they hadn’t arrived.
What we did
We started with the founder’s biggest manual task, arranging installations, and handled it with AI agents and automation, then covered warehouse, logistics and customer updates. Inefficient steps were removed instead of automated, which saved money. Now any part of the operation that stops working alerts the founder straight away.
Result
No new hire needed: automating the flow first removed the need for the extra role. A complicated setup became a much smaller one, designed so one person can run the whole operation, with no human in the loop for routine work and very few changes as the business grows.

12Questions

Questions brands ask us.

We already have automations. Why does it still feel like we’re doing everything?

Because most setups automate the steps but not the watching. Someone still has to notice when a flow stops, remember the edge cases and fix things when apps change. We take over that part too, so the work actually leaves your plate.

What exactly do you build?

A custom system built around how your business works, so no two builds look the same. It can cover the whole flow: from the moment an order comes in, through warehouse, delivery, installation and customer updates, to asking for a review and checking that your service landed well. We only automate the parts that are worth it, and simplify or remove the rest.

Do we need to have automations already?

No. Most clients start with an audit and a first build. If you already have automations or an AI support agent, we start with a health check instead.

Who owns what you build?

You do. Everything is built in your own Shopify, helpdesk and automation accounts, with documentation your team can read.

What happens when something breaks?

Our checks flag it, we fix it, and you get a short note explaining what happened and what changed. Fixes for things we monitor are included in your plan.

Which tools do you work with?

Shopify and Shopify Flow, Gorgias, Klaviyo, n8n, Make and Zapier, plus custom AI agents where off-the-shelf tools fall short.

Will AI make this expensive to run?

Not when it’s designed well. We only use AI where it does real work, choose the smallest model that does the job reliably, and keep calls to a minimum, so your monthly running costs stay low.

Can we cancel the monthly plan?

Yes, with 30 days’ notice. Because everything lives in your accounts with documentation, you keep it all.

Tell us what you still check by hand.

A free 30-minute call. We’ll tell you honestly what can run on its own, what should be simplified, and what isn’t worth automating. The in-depth review of your setup happens in the paid audit.
